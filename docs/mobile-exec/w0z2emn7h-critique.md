# Completeness critique — what is missing or wrong from the owner's point of view

Verified against the tree at `d:/My_Works/ERP/ERPCLEAN2` (`develop`, `53343e96`). New repo findings are cited; business judgements are marked as such.

---

## (1) Concrete gaps

### A. Commercial reality — the plan has no money in it

- **There is no cost, no budget, and no return statement anywhere in ~25,000 words. → NEW SECTION, before §1 Naming: "What this costs and what it buys."**
  The owner's first question is not "what shall we call it" but "what does this cost me and what do I get back". The plan must state, in one table: build cost (13.5 developer-weeks at whatever the internal rate is, plus 4–6 more for iOS), one-off external costs (Google Play developer registration, Apple Developer Program annually if iOS, a code-signing keystore in escrow, a lawyer pass on `dist/bundle/LICENSE.txt` which still carries the banner "TEMPLATE — NOT YET A LICENCE. REPLACE BEFORE SHIPPING TO ANY CLIENT"), recurring costs (Firebase, which is free at this volume but is a Google account we own and must keep alive; per-release rebuild and redistribution across every self-hosted client), and the return, which is honestly not revenue but two things: purchase orders released in hours instead of days, and a differentiator when selling OrbixERP against the next competitor. If the app is intended to be **billable** — a per-user or per-organisation add-on — say so now, because that changes licensing, the LICENSE.txt rewrite, and whether an executive can be locked out for non-payment. Nothing in the backend enforces any licence or subscription today (grep finds no licence-key or subscription machinery), so "we will charge for it" is itself unbuilt work.

- **"One full-time developer plus part-time review" is a single point of failure on a product that had two production outages in one day on 2026-08-15. → §Architecture, Roadmap & Delivery §3, as a new "Who builds it, and what they stop doing" paragraph.**
  The same person who would build this is the person who restores the live client when 1.8.0 crash-loops. Thirteen and a half weeks of mobile work is thirteen and a half weeks of not hardening production, and the plan never says which loses. State the capacity rule explicitly: production stability and client-blocking defects preempt the mobile app, always; the roadmap is elapsed-weeks-if-uninterrupted and should be presented to the owner as "roughly one calendar quarter, assuming a normal rate of interruption". Also name the skills gap honestly: this repo has never shipped an iOS build (no `ios/` directory in `pos_app/`), has never run a Flutter test in CI (`.github/workflows/` has only `backend-ci.yml` and `web-ci.yml`), and has never published to any app store. The first three of those are learned on this project, at this project's cost.

- **The release keystore is the one artefact that cannot be recovered, and the plan treats it as a to-do. → §Architecture §1.3, rule 7, expanded; and §5 Distribution.**
  Lose the Android signing keystore and you cannot ship an update to an installed app — ever; every user must uninstall and reinstall from a new listing. `pos_app/android/app/build.gradle.kts:37` still signs release with the debug keystore, so this has never been done properly for OrbixPOS either. Add: the keystore and its passwords go into the owner's own password manager and a sealed offline copy, not only the developer's machine; the same applies to the Firebase project owner account, the Play publisher account, and the `orbix.*` domain if one is bought. This is business continuity, not engineering hygiene — if the developer disappears, these five items decide whether the app can ever be updated again.

### B. Support burden — the section that does not exist

- **There is no support model at all. → NEW SECTION after §Distribution: "Support and the 9pm phone call."**
  Nine executives with a phone app will generate support contacts, and every one currently lands on one person with no triage. Define: a single in-app "Get help" button that opens WhatsApp to a named number with a pre-filled diagnostic block (app version, server host, active company, last `X-Request-Id` — which the client already generates durably at `pos_app/lib/core/api/api_client.dart:51-53` and which is the single fastest way to find the matching server log line); a documented response expectation ("same business day, not same hour"); and an explicit statement that the app is supported only on the handsets recorded in the fleet register. Without this, "the app is broken" arrives at 9pm as a voice note with no version number, and the first thirty minutes of every incident is spent establishing which build the person is running.

- **"A manager loses their phone at 9pm" is solved cryptographically and not operationally. → §Authentication & Security §6, new subsection 6.4 "The lost-phone runbook", and a new file `docs/ops/mobile-lost-device.md`.**
  The plan has `sessions_revoked_at` (B17/V107) and self-service revoke, which is the right machinery, but it never answers: who does the manager phone at 9pm; what if the person holding `USER.MANAGE` is asleep, or is the person who lost the phone; what if the client has exactly one administrator, which most SME installs do; and what happens in the fifteen minutes before the access token expires. Write the runbook as five numbered steps with a named human at each: (1) manager calls the client's ERP administrator, or, out of hours, our support number; (2) administrator opens the web client on any laptop and revokes the device, or, if unavailable, we do it directly on the box; (3) the manager's password is reset, forcing a fresh login everywhere; (4) if the handset was the administrator's own, there must be a second administrator account, so **a standing rule: no client goes live on mobile with a single administrator**; (5) log it, and check the audit trail for any approval decided after the loss was noticed. Add the honest caveat: for up to fifteen minutes after revocation a thief with the app already open can still read cached figures and, if the step-up ticket design has not shipped, potentially approve.

- **Per-executive onboarding has a chicken-and-egg problem the plan does not see. → §Authentication & Security §3.3, step 1.**
  Pairing requires an administrator to mint a code in the web client. At a typical client the "administrator" is the owner — the same person who is trying to install the app, on the phone, possibly for the first time. Add either a printed pairing sheet produced at install time by the deployment engineer (host, fingerprint, one code per named executive, valid 7 days), or a first-run path where a user who can log in with a password on the box can self-pair without an administrator. Without this, every new executive is a scheduled call with us.

### C. Training and adoption — absent

- **No training plan, no documentation deliverable, no adoption measure. → NEW SECTION at the end: "Rollout, training and what success looks like."**
  `docs/user-manual/` has twelve chapters and 7,594 lines; the plan never mentions writing a thirteenth. Add: a one-page laminated card per executive (open the app, what the three tabs are, how to approve, what to do if the numbers look wrong, who to call); a twenty-minute face-to-face session per person, in their office, on their handset, in which they approve one real low-value purchase order while we watch; a chapter `docs/user-manual/12-mobile.md`; and a written acceptance criterion the owner signs off — for example, "in week 1, the GM approves at least five real purchase orders from the phone and opens the app on at least four days". If that criterion is not met, the problem is not features, and building phase 3 will not fix it. Measuring adoption requires nothing new server-side: the audit trail already records the channel once §A.7's `detail` extension ships.

- **The app requires per-client configuration work that nobody has been assigned. → §Approvals §A.8 and §Roadmap §7 as a new Open Decision.**
  The plan correctly finds that there is no `GENERAL_MANAGER` role bundle and that approval routing matches a role code frozen onto a policy step. What it never schedules is the human work that follows: for each client, someone must decide the approval bands, write the policies, name the new role in the steps, and assign it to the right people. Sales-order approvals are off by default per company (`sales_settings.so_approval_enabled` defaults false), so at most clients the inbox will be empty on day one for a second reason nobody has looked at. Budget two to four hours of configuration per client, put a named person on it, and make "approval policies configured and tested with one real document" a go-live gate, exactly as the SAM Electronix go-live gate was written.

### D. Does the report set answer "how is my business doing"? Not yet

- **The catalogue is a finance department's view, not an owner's. → §Reports & Dashboards, new §1.5 "The six questions an owner actually asks."**
  Fourteen proposed endpoints deliver sales, margin, cash, AR, AP, stock, budget, production, payroll and CRM — and still do not directly answer the questions an owner asks in a Tanzanian trading business. Add them as named tiles: (a) *Did the money that I earned actually arrive?* — profit for the month beside the movement in cash and the movement in debtors, on one line, because a business can be profitable and broke; (b) *Is anyone stealing from me?* — see the next bullet; (c) *Am I about to run out of my best sellers?* — a stock-out risk count over the top-selling SKUs, which no endpoint in the catalogue provides; (d) *What do I have to pay, and when?* — the plan gives AP outstanding but not "due in the next 7 days", which is the number that decides whether to bank today's takings; (e) *Is my debt getting worse or better?* — AR ageing is a snapshot in every proposed DTO; the owner needs the 90-plus figure against last month, not just today; (f) *What did each branch manager do about the thing I asked them last week?* — out of scope, but say so.

- **There is no exceptions feed, and it is probably the highest-value screen in the whole app. → §Reports & Dashboards, new §2.16 "Exceptions — things that look wrong."**
  Everything in the plan tells the owner what happened. Nothing tells him what looks wrong. The data already exists for: till variances (the z-read path), voided invoices, discounts approved above the cashier ceiling (the discount-authorisation guard already records a refusal code), sales at or below cost (ADR-0060 exists), negative stock (the negative-stock toggle shipped 2026-08-01), backdated or weekend postings, and manual journals over a threshold. One screen, one server-composed endpoint, one line each: "3 tills out by more than TZS 20,000 yesterday · 2 invoices voided after fiscalisation · 1 sale below cost". For an owner running nine branches he cannot visit, this is the reason to keep the app on the home screen; the morning brief is the reason he installs it.

- **The TRA compliance number is missing entirely, and it is the one that keeps a Tanzanian owner awake. → §Reports & Dashboards §2.1, new row; and §4 as a new proposed endpoint.**
  Verified: `FiscalReceiptStatus` (`backend/src/main/java/com/erp/modules/sales/domain/enums/FiscalReceiptStatus.java`) has `PENDING`, `ISSUED`, `FAILED`, `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `VOID`, and the only endpoints are per-invoice (`GET/POST /api/v1/sales-invoices/uid/{uid}/fiscal-receipt`, gated `FISCAL.VIEW` / `FISCAL.MANAGE`). There is **no aggregate anywhere**. So "how many of yesterday's 412 receipts did not reach TRA" — a question with a direct financial penalty attached — cannot be answered on the phone or on the desktop. Add a proposed counts endpoint returning issued / failed / not-configured for a date range and branch, and put it on the brief as a chip that is invisible when everything is `ISSUED` and red when it is not. This is a stronger candidate for the MVP than the branch league table.

### E. Multi-company owners — half handled

- **The plan handles multiple companies inside one organisation, and silently assumes an owner never has two organisations or two servers. → §Architecture §1.4 (state) and §Authentication §3, as a new "Multiple businesses, multiple servers" subsection, and Open Decision #11.**
  Org-as-tenant (ADR-0062) means one login belongs to one organisation. A Tanzanian owner with a hardware shop on our production box and a second business installed separately on his own server has two organisations, two hostnames, two certificates and two identities — and the plan's `sessionProvider` is `AsyncNotifier<Session?>`, singular. That is a phase-0 architectural decision, not a later feature: the pairing store must be a list, the session model must be a map keyed by pairing, the token store must namespace by server, and the UI needs a top-level account switcher above the company picker. Retrofitting this after phase 4 means rewriting the auth layer. Ask the owner directly in week 1: "do any of your customers, or you, own more than one business that is on a *separate* OrbixERP installation?" If yes, build the list from day one; if no, write the assumption down so the next person knows why it is singular.

- **Group totals in one currency have no rate source at group level, and the plan's answer stops at a warning flag. → §Reports §4, N1 implementation intent; and §Risk R7.**
  Verified: `CurrencyRate` (`backend/src/main/java/com/erp/modules/fx/domain/entity/CurrencyRate.java`) is strictly per-company — its javadoc states "Strict per-company tenancy: every row carries `company_id` … No `company_id IS NULL` global rows in v1 (D-2)" — with an effective-dated as-of lookup and a fixed direction convention (`base = face × rate`). So the FX machinery to translate a group total *does* exist, contrary to the flat statement that "no report FX-translates"; what does not exist is a group-scoped rate. The brief's DTO should therefore carry `presentationCurrency`, `rateDate` and `rateSourceCompanyUid`, translate using the presentation company's own effective-dated rates, and render the untranslated per-company lines directly beneath the group figure so the CFO can always see what was converted at what rate. Also warn the owner that a translated group total will legitimately differ from the sum of the companies' own books once FX revaluation runs have posted — that is accounting, not a bug, and it must be said before he sees it, not after.

### F. Connectivity — right instinct, wrong depth

- **Offline is designed for approvals and barely designed for the reports the executive actually opens. → §Reports & Dashboards, new §6 "Data, bundles and the road to Dodoma."**
  The refusal to queue approvals offline is correct and well argued. What is missing is the everyday Tanzanian pattern: a metered data bundle, 2G in parts of the country, and an executive who opens the app in a car. Add a stated data budget — target under 5 MB per user per month for normal use, which the 8 KB brief plus ETag revalidation comfortably meets, and which makes the PDF share flow (a multi-megabyte download) something the confirm sheet must price honestly. Add a "low data mode" that suppresses the automatic morning fetch on mobile data. And add the missing channel: an **SMS or WhatsApp digest of the morning brief**. The plan rejects SMS as an authentication factor, which is right, and then never revisits it as a *delivery* channel — yet a single daily text reading "Sales 48.2M, cash 154M, 3 approvals waiting" reaches the owner in a place where the app cannot, costs almost nothing, and is exactly what this audience already uses. It needs a server-side sender and is out of v1 scope, but it should be named as the phase 6 candidate rather than silently absent.

- **If the client's server is down, the app is dead and nobody knows. → §Distribution §5, new "Fleet health" paragraph.**
  There is no monitoring of client boxes today, and actuator sits on port 9090 behind the edge. The first symptom of a down server will be the GM's phone showing a stale brief, and he will conclude the app is broken. Add a minimal fleet register (client, host, version, contact) and either a daily automated probe of each client's `/api/v1/health` from our side, or accept and state that the app cannot distinguish "your server is off" from "our app is broken" and make the diagnosing probe's wording carry that burden.

### G. Privacy and legal — a section-sized hole

- **Nothing addresses the Personal Data Protection Act 2022, and both app stores will demand what does not exist. → NEW SECTION: "Privacy, consent and the law."**
  The app will collect and transmit device identifiers, IP addresses, push tokens and last-seen timestamps, and will display payroll totals and named debtors. Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act 2022 and its Commission bring registration and notification duties for controllers and processors; whether we are a controller, a processor, or both, per client, is a question for a lawyer, not for this plan. Independently, Google Play and the App Store both require a published privacy policy at a stable URL and a data-safety declaration before a listing goes live, and neither exists. Add three concrete deliverables to phase 5: a privacy policy hosted on a domain we control, a data-processing note in the client contract naming exactly what leaves their server (push metadata to Google, nothing else, and only if push ships), and the lawyer pass that `dist/bundle/LICENSE.txt` has been waiting for. None of this blocks development; all of it blocks a store listing, so it must start in parallel and not in week 12.

- **The app quietly turns into an employee-monitoring tool and nobody has told the employees. → §Authentication & Security §7, new paragraph; and the training section.**
  Stamping device id, IP and app version into `audit_log` (§A.7) means the owner can see where and when a branch manager approved a purchase order. That is defensible and useful, and it must be disclosed in writing to the people it applies to, before it ships, not discovered afterwards. One paragraph in the staff handbook and one line on the training card.

### H. Is the naming recommendation actually good?

- **OrbixHQ is a defensible choice reached by over-weighting the wrong criterion, and there is a specific problem with it. → §1.3, replacing the "one honest objection" paragraph.**
  The analysis scores family shape, label length and colour contrast in great detail, and under-weights the one thing that matters commercially: the name is spoken to a *branch manager* as often as to an owner. "HQ" means head office — it names the place branch managers are not, and the plan's own persona matrix insists every screen be tested as `BRANCH_MANAGER`. If branch managers get logins in v1, and they do, then the name is subtly wrong for a third of its users on day one. Two honest options: keep **OrbixHQ** and accept that branch managers are secondary users of an executive tool, which is a legitimate product decision; or take **OrbixBoard**, whose two meanings both survive a branch manager holding it. My recommendation is to keep OrbixHQ — it is short, unambiguous and sells well — but to record the branch-manager reading as the accepted cost, rather than to argue it away. Two process points belong in the same section. First, roughly four thousand words on naming for an app with nine users is an inversion of effort; the naming decision is a ten-minute conversation and only **§1.5, the bundle id, is genuinely irreversible** — that is the one item that must not be decided casually, and it needs the owner to answer whether he holds an `orbix.*` domain at all, given every shipped artefact today says `net.otapp`. Second, the plan never asks whether "Orbix" is the customer-facing brand or an internal product name under otapp; if there is any chance the client-facing brand changes, the bundle id must be `net.otapp.*`, which survives a rebrand, and the store display name can change freely afterwards.

### I. Is the MVP too big? Yes — in prerequisites, not in features

- **v0.1 as written cannot ship without a private-CA pairing flow, a permission-seed edit, per-client approval-policy configuration, five backend items, a hardware-key token store and a brand-new CI lane. → §Roadmap §3, replacing the MVP definition.**
  Every one of those is a place the schedule can break, and they are all in front of the first build the owner ever sees. Insert a **phase 0.5 pilot, roughly three weeks, with zero new backend, zero migrations and zero seed changes**: point the app at the existing production box, whose Caddy root is already compiled into the POS client (`kProdCaddyRootCa`, valid to 2036), log in with a real account, and show two things read-only — the approvals inbox for the one active company, and yesterday's sales. No decide button. No QR pairing. No cross-company loop. That build answers the only question that actually matters before spending a quarter: *will he open it?* If he opens it on four days out of five, everything after is justified; if he does not, no amount of phase 3 will change that, and we will have learned it for three weeks instead of thirteen. Then move the decide button into phase 1, where the safety work (B7, step-up, the read-back path) belongs, and push the cross-company inbox (B4) to phase 2, since most clients have exactly one company today.

- **Conversely, the MVP is missing the thing that makes an app get opened. → same section.**
  An approvals inbox that is usually empty is an app that gets deleted in week three. Approvals are episodic; numbers are daily. The MVP must carry at least one figure — yesterday's sales for the active company, summed client-side from `/bi/sales-by-branch`, which exists today and needs no backend work at all — even though the plan defers every number to phase 3. One tile, zero new endpoints, and the app now has a reason to be opened on the days nothing needs approving.

### J. Quiet assumptions worth writing on the wall

- **Five assumptions the plan makes without stating them. → NEW SECTION at the end: "What this plan assumes."**
  First, that the owner *wants* approvals to move faster — sometimes a bottleneck is deliberate, and a purchase order that sits for two days is a control, not a defect; ask before optimising it away. Second, that the executives carry modern Android handsets with working biometrics, Play services and free storage — the entire authentication design rests on StrongBox and hardware-bound keys, so confirm the actual four or five handsets before designing around them, not after. Third, that the numbers are already right — the plan is admirably careful about not *presenting* wrong numbers, but an owner whose group total disagrees with his accountant's report by forty thousand shillings will stop trusting the app permanently and will not come back; add a pre-launch reconciliation in which, for five consecutive business days, a human ties every brief figure to the desktop report and signs it off before the app is handed over. Fourth, that one binary serves every client while the update channel is an APK served from each client's own box — which means a fleet inventory we do not have and a rebuild-and-redistribute step per release for every client. Fifth, that the client's own administrator will do the configuration, the pairing and the first-line support; if that person does not exist at a given client, the app is not deployable there and that should be a documented prerequisite, not a surprise.

## (2) Executive summary — for the opening of the plan

> **A phone app for the people who own the business.**
>
> You asked for something that lets you and your senior managers see how the business is doing, and approve what needs approving, without opening a laptop. This document is the plan for that app. We propose calling it **OrbixHQ** — your head office, in your pocket — sitting alongside OrbixERP on the desk and OrbixPOS at the till.
>
> It does two things. It answers *how is my business doing* — yesterday's sales across every branch, your margin, the cash you hold, who owes you and how badly, and what looks wrong — on one screen that loads in under a second on a poor connection. And it lets you **approve or send back purchase orders and sales orders from anywhere**, with your fingerprint, so nothing waits three days on your desk. It does not do data entry; that stays where it belongs.
>
> We recommend building it in stages. In about three weeks you get a first version to carry for a fortnight: your approvals and yesterday's sales, read-only. That tells us whether you actually open it, before we spend a quarter on the rest. Approving from the phone follows, then the full morning picture, then group totals across all your companies, then alerts that tell you an approval is waiting. A hardened version is roughly one calendar quarter of work.
>
> Three things need your decision before anyone writes code: whether iPhones are in scope, whether we buy proper web addresses and certificates for every installation, and who inside each business is responsible for setting it up and answering the phone when someone loses theirs.

*(248 words.)*
